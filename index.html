Forest soil microbial ecology

The hidden life beneath the forest floor

Nobuhiko Shigyo, Ph.D. ／ 執行 宣彦Lecturer, Graduate School of Horticulture, Chiba University

Beneath the ground we walk on every day lies an invisible world teeming with microbial life. I study how bacteria and fungi are distributed in forest soils, how they change across space and time, and how they shape the functioning of forest ecosystems.

Using a multifaceted approach that combines molecular experiments, field observations, manipulation studies and statistical modelling, I ask how soil microbial communities are assembled and what they do for the forest.

Selected publication

Soil microbial identity explains home-field advantage for litter decomposition

New Phytologist 243: 2141–2156 (2024)

Leaf litter often decomposes faster in the forest it came from than elsewhere. By combining reciprocal litter transplants with manipulations of the soil microbial community, this study shows that the identity of the microbes, rather than the soil environment alone, drives this home-field advantage.

Highlighted with a Commentary by Ashley Keiser in New Phytologist: “Putting soil microbes on first: Identifying the engines of home-field advantage in litter decomposition”. Read the commentary
